Reviewer's Comments:

I have read with interest the manuscript referenced AAS64879, titled
"Physics-Informed Priors Improve Gravitational-Wave Constraints on
Neutron-Star Matter" by Magnall et al. This study presents a method to
produce physics informed priors connecting tidal deformability and chirp
mass and use a spectral parameterization for the nuclear equation of
state construction and inference. The results presented are of interest
to several people in this particular research area and to people from
several areas in astronomy and nuclear physics. However, the text could
be improved in some places to improve the clarity for readers. I request
the following revisions, after which I could recommend publication of the
manuscript in the Astrophysical Journal Letters.

typo: abstract: at the events measured chirp mass -> for events measured
with chirp mass ... ?

Abstract: lines 19-21: "Given their ability to improve the astrophysical
inference of binary mergers involving neutron stars, we advocate for
these physics-informed priors to be used as standard in the literature
and provide open-source code for reproducibility and adaptation of the
method."  >> too strong a statement. The authors should remove it or
modify it to indicate how based on their results, these priors could get
reliable measurements. Providing open source code for reproducibility is
a common practice now and therefore, that part of the sentence does not
add any value to the abstract.

"The above prior assumptions are overly conservative. Basic physical
principles such as causality, consistency with nuclear-physics
experiments, and perturbative QCD calculations, imply a strong
correlation between M and \Lambda in neutron-star binaries."
>> citation?

In the abstract could you also quote the 90% upper limit in addition to
the median + 90% confidence interval from your study? Then it will be
easy to compare with the previous upper limit 720.

"We show a significant improvement on the measurement ..."
>>This line could go in the conclusions not introduction

"When including observed rates as the prior odds, we find an odds ratio of 0.33"
>> Odds ratio comparing what over what? i.e., it's not clear whether the
   BNS is in the numerator or denominator

"We describe details of the method and implementation in the next Section."
>> Do you mean Methods section, or Section 2? Mention which section
   specifically.

Section 2.1:

"We use the probability distributions described in the previous Section
as priors on the source-frame chirp mass and effective tidal
deformability."
>> Could you be specific about how the priors were constructed?

"where the fitting coefficients are a = ?57 (?23) ..."
>> It's not clear what the numbers in the brackets are.

"We sample directly in source-frame chirp mass, mass ratio and the two
tidal deformability parameters \Lambda and \delta \Lambda"
>> In the next paragraph the authors say that they do not have a prior on
   \delta \Lambda. Can you clarify how you don't have priors on \delta
   \Lambda but also say that you sample in that parameter?

The physics informed priors imposes a hard cut on the tidal deformability
excluding values below ~200, which then translates to the
posterior. Since this prior essentially eliminates a large chunk of soft
equation of states, could you emphasize how the calculations you present
for your physics-informed priors translates to the cut and provide an
physical explanation for why eliminating low Lambdas is a valid
assumption for GW170817?

Fig 3 caption: "the dashed lines show 90% credible intervals of the
priors of chirp mass and tidal deformability ..."
>> This line is confusing. Do you just mean the physics-informed priors
   for the corresponding posteriors?

Fig 4 caption: "The posterior using uniform priors from the LVK is reported in blue."
>> maybe you can add that they assume a BNS merger?

In the conclusions, you quote the 90% upper limit from the LVK analysis
and compare that to a 90% credible interval from your physics informed
prior analysis. Can you quote the 90% upper limit for your analysis as
well, so that it's easier to compare?
