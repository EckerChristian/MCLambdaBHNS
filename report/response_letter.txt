# Abstract: lines 19-21: "Given their ability to improve the
# astrophysical inference of binary mergers involving neutron stars, we
# advocate for these physics-informed priors to be used as standard in
# the literature and provide open-source code for reproducibility and
# adaptation of the method."  >> too strong a statement. The authors
# should remove it or modify it to indicate how based on their results,
# these priors could get reliable measurements. Providing open source
# code for reproducibility is a common practice now and therefore, that
# part of the sentence does not add any value to the abstract.

  This statement includes an objective and a subjective component. The
  objective part is true, and is shown in the paper - i.e., that the
  physics-informed priors used in this paper improve the astrophysical
  inference. We therefore understand that the Referee is arguing that
  advocating for using these priors as standard in the literature is the
  part of the sentence that is too strong. We respectfully disagree with
  this sentiment, and note that it may fall outside of the scope of the
  Referee's role. Firstly, our manuscript shows that the physics-informed
  priors improve astrophysical inference, and they are completely within
  the spirit of Bayesian methodology; i.e., that one starts with their
  prior belief about how systems behave, and then uses data to update
  those beliefs. Therefore, starting with uniform priors incorporates
  nothing about our prior knowledge of systems, and so we do advocate for
  these priors to become standard in the literature. Secondly, there is
  nothing objectively wrong with this statement, but instead the Referee
  may be offering a stylistic comment, which we respectfully acknowledge
  but do not share. Finally, we find that "advocating" the use of a
  technique or procedure should be something that scientists should be
  able to do. It is then up to their peers' judgement - which is
  subjective - to accept or not the suggestion on the basis of the
  evidence we have provided and which is objective.
  
  With respect to the comment about providing open-source code, we again
  respectfully disagree with the Referee that this does not add value to
  the abstract. The implementation of these priors are complicated for a
  number of reasons. We are the developers of both the code that
  generates the priors (which is computationally expensive), and also the
  original developers of the parameter-estimation software. We believe
  making the code open-source is important for the community, to the
  point of being included in the abstract.

# In the abstract could you also quote the 90% upper limit in addition to
# the median + 90% confidence interval from your study? Then it will be
# easy to compare with the previous upper limit 720.

  In the abstract, we quote the upper limit on Lambda for the original
  (i.e., the LVK) results because those results are also consistent with
  zero, implying the measurement is only that of an upper limit. On the
  other hand, our results rule out Lambda = 0 with a large degree of
  confidence, meaning that a 90% upper limit is not a relevant quantity
  to quote. For this reason, it would be misleading to quote a 90% upper
  limit in the abstract for our results; if we were to do this in the
  abstract, it would further require a couple of sentences explaining
  this result, which would unnecessarily bloat the abstract.

# "We sample directly in source-frame chirp mass, mass ratio and the two
# tidal deformability parameters \Lambda and \delta \Lambda" >> In the
# next paragraph the authors say that they do not have a prior on \delta
# \Lambda. Can you clarify how you don't have priors on \delta \Lambda
# but also say that you sample in that parameter?

  We thank the Referee for pointing out how this is confusing. We sample
  over lambda_tilde and delta_lambda when we perform parameter
  estimation, but we do not construct a "physics inspired prior" for
  delta_lambda as we do for lambda_tilde. The prior on delta_lambda is
  set to be uniform during parameter estimation. We have amended the
  manuscript to make this explicitly clear.


# The physics informed priors imposes a hard cut on the tidal
# deformability excluding values below ~200, which then translates to the
# posterior. Since this prior essentially eliminates a large chunk of
# soft equation of states, could you emphasize how the calculations you
# present for your physics-informed priors translates to the cut and
# provide an physical explanation for why eliminating low Lambdas is a
# valid assumption for GW170817?

  We thank the Referee for raising this point. The exclusion of the
  small-Lambda part of the distribution is the combination of all the
  constraints that are imposed on the EOSs we construct. However, the
  most significant of such constraint is that coming from pQCD, that
  "filters" out very soft EOSs as they would lead to rather small sound
  speeds at small densities but then superluminal ones at higher
  densities in order to match the asymptotic pQCD constraint. We have
  added a statement on this in the revised version.
