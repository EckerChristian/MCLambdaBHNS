Abstract: lines 19-21: "Given their ability to improve the astrophysical
inference of binary mergers involving neutron stars, we advocate for
these physics-informed priors to be used as standard in the literature
and provide open-source code for reproducibility and adaptation of the
method."  >> too strong a statement. The authors should remove it or
modify it to indicate how based on their results, these priors could get
reliable measurements. Providing open source code for reproducibility is
a common practice now and therefore, that part of the sentence does not
add any value to the abstract.



This statement includes an objective and a subjective component. The objective part is true, and is shown in the paper – i.e., that the physics-informed priors used in this paper improve the astrophysical inference.
We therefore understand that the Referee is arguing that advocating for using these priors as standard in the literature is the part of the sentence that is too strong.
We respectfully disagree with this sentiment, and note that it may fall outside of the scope of the Referee's role. 
Firstly, our manuscript shows that the physics-informed priors improve astrophysical inference, and they are completely within the spirit of Bayesian methodology; i.e., that one starts with their prior belief about how systems behave, and then uses data to update those beliefs.
Therefore, starting with uniform priors incorporates nothing about our prior knowledge of systems, and so we do advocate for these priors to become standard in the literature.
Secondly, there is nothing objectively wrong with this statement, but instead the Referee may be offering a stylistic comment, which we respectfully acknowledge but do not share. 




With respect to the comment about providing open-source code, we again respectfully disagree with the Referee that this does not add value to the abstract.
The implementation of these priors are complicated for a number of reasons.
We are the developers of both the code that generates the priors (which is computationally expensive), and also the original developers of the parameter-estimation software.
We believe making the code open source is important for the community, to the point of being included in the abstract.

In the abstract could you also quote the 90% upper limit in addition to
the median + 90% confidence interval from your study? Then it will be
easy to compare with the previous upper limit 720.

In the abstract, we quote the upper limit on Lambda for the original (i.e., the LVK) results because those results are also consistent with zero, implying the measurement is only that of an upper limit.
On the other hand, our results rule out Lambda = 0 with a large degree of confidence, meaning that a 90% upper limit is not a relevant quantity to quote.
For this reason, it would be misleading to quote a 90% upper limit in the abstract for our results; if we were to do this in the abstract, it would further require a couple of sentences explaining this result, which would unnecessarily bloat the abstract.
